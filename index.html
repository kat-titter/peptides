Frontier biotech, first-class mail

For $54 to $74, a vial of copper peptide powder comes in the post78, labelled “research use only”. Peptides are the frontier of biotech, and anyone can order one. They are short chains of amino acids, the messages the body already sends: insulin, GLP-1 and oxytocin are peptides.1 Many work like a key, fitting a receptor on a cell and switching it on at tiny (nanomolar) doses, then breaking down into plain amino acids. Their weakness is staying power. A bare peptide lasts minutes in blood and is lost in the gut, so semaglutide was given a fatty tail, and now it lasts a week.18 Does a vial in the post work in people, and how would you know? This page asks that of each peptide it follows.

A peptide fits a receptor the way the body’s own hormone does

Most drugs are small molecules, built to sit in a pocket. Many of the body's most important switches have no pocket: hormone receptors such as GLP-1's hold their hormone across a long interface, and protein meets protein over flat, wide faces. A peptide fits because it is the message those switches evolved to hold.1,19

The name. Emil Fischer joined two glycines in 1902 and named the product a peptide, after peptone, digested protein, from the Greek for "cooked" (standard history, not retrieved here). The name says what the thing is: a piece of a protein, the size digestion leaves behind.

The bond. Join the acid end of one amino acid to the amine end of the next and a water molecule leaves: that link is the peptide bond (Figure 19). Each amino acid in a chain is a residue, a bead on a string (bond and term: standard chemistry, not retrieved here). Chemists use peptide loosely for short chains; the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) draws its legal line at 40.2 With twenty kinds of bead, a 15-bead chain like BPC-157 is one of 2015≈3.3×1019 possible messages.

Each peptide on this page acts at its own address in the cell

Every cell is a house with a lock on every door, and peptides are the keys the body cuts for itself. They knock at the door (GLP-1 at its receptor) or hold the furniture still (Tβ4 on actin,94 elamipretide on cardiolipin). Some walk right in. MOTS-c talks to the kitchen and then the office (the mitochondrion, then the nucleus). Others catch a bus, the blood protein albumin, and stay in town for a week. Each visit starts a mechanism of action, its MoA: the chain from where a drug binds to the change a body shows. Semaglutide’s chain ends in a long programme of randomised trials, BPC-157’s runs mostly through rats, and MOTS-c’s rests on one trial of a close copy, in 20 people. Every chain starts at an address on this cell.

GLP-1: the class that worked in people, and the money that followed

After a meal your gut texts “enough”. That text is GLP-1, a gut hormone: it tells the pancreas to release insulin only when blood sugar is high, slows the stomach and makes you feel full.19 The text self-deletes in about a minute and a half. Chemists shielded it from the enzyme that deletes it, gave it a fatty tail — and now it lasts a week. The first drug came from a Gila-monster venom peptide (1992);25 semaglutide and tirzepatide followed, and in 2026 retatrutide reached 28% weight loss.24,28 In 2025 those drugs earned Novo DKK 206 bn and Lilly $36.5 bn. This chain was followed all the way to people, and every chapter after it is measured against it.

the threat inside the class: a pill that is not a peptide

Patients on orforglipron lose less weight than on the injectables: 11.2% of body weight at the top dose, against 2.1% on placebo.27 The peptides’ moat is potency per molecule and the albumin trick; the pill’s moat is the bottle. Both will share a market that Novo now guides down 5 to 13% on price.6

What proof in people looks like. Every rung can fail, which is why so few molecules finish. The GLP-1 class climbed all of them.

BPC-157 and MOTS-c: clean mechanisms, almost no proof in people

BPC-157 and MOTS-c have the best-drawn mechanisms on the page: a sequence, a receptor, a cell that changed. Rats and mice supplied nearly all of it. Against GLP-1’s record, BPC-157’s only randomised trial in people found no effect, and MOTS-c’s only human study tested a close copy.

BPC-157: a stomach fragment tested almost only in rats

BPC-157 is a 15-bead message cut from a stomach protein, and it appears to work from outside cells. On blood-vessel cells it raises VEGFR2, the receptor for a growth signal, so vessels grow;29 on tendon cells it raises the growth-hormone receptor, so tendons knit.31,30 Nearly every paper on it since 1992 came from one lab, in Zagreb,17 and sellers pitch it for tendons, gut and joints. Its rodent record is large and consistent. In people, the one fair test (53 patients, two weeks) heard nothing above the noise.13

The evidence in numbers. PubMed holds about 200 BPC-157 studies.17 The FDA found five in people: a randomised trial, null and published only as an abstract,13 a placebo-controlled tolerability study in 24 healthy subjects, and three clinic reports of 2 to 17 people each with no control group.17 Its adverse-event database held three reports to December 2025, it identified no cancer study at all, and the repeat-dose and DNA-damage studies it did find were animal studies that did not cover how the compounded product would be used.13

what the FDA concluded, conflicts, doping

What the FDA concluded. Its scientists found the legal criteria "weigh against" adding BPC-157 to the compounding list: no evidence it works in ulcerative colitis, too little human safety data to characterise the risk, a risk of immune reactions to impurities and clumps in the powder (certificates at 97.8% and 98.3% purity, no endotoxin test), and animal toxicology that does not cover the product as it would be sold.13 Its advisory committee voted the other way on 23 July 2026,16 and the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) put BPC-157 on its enforcement-discretion list for injection under the skin or into muscle, by mouth and on the skin.14 A 2025 review of muscle and joint use reached the same place from the clinic: promising animal data, no controlled human trial.33

Conflicts. In February 2026 Undark and STAT reported that Sikirić has been named on BPC-157 patent applications since 1989. He is listed as an owner of PharmaCotherapia, which sponsored a 2015 trial whose data were submitted and then withdrawn from the registry, and as CEO of Diagen, which says it owns a patent on a stable form that is "currently for sale". None of this appeared on the papers they reviewed.17 That is no evidence the biology is wrong. It is a reason to want a second lab — and there is one, small, in Taiwan.

Doping. The World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) named BPC-157 as an example under S0, non-approved substances, from 1 January 2022.10

MOTS-c: a hormone written in mitochondrial DNA

Mitochondria, the cell's power plants, keep a tiny notebook of their own DNA. On a page everyone took for scaffolding, the 12S rRNA gene, hides a 16-bead message: MOTS-c, found in 2015.34 Exercise prints it. In the cell’s fluid it brakes the folate cycle, which switches on AMPK, the cell’s fuel gauge, so muscle burns fuel.34,35 When glucose runs short it walks to the office — the nucleus — and helps switch on antioxidant genes.36 It travels in the blood and, in mice, falls with age and helps insulin work.34,37

Why it matters anyway. MOTS-c is the clearest case of mitochondria talking back to the nucleus with peptides, the one shown to enter the nucleus itself.52,53 It makes exercise a drug target with a messenger the body already makes. That is a research programme, not yet a product.

Nose and skin: doors that need no needle

Semax goes up the nose; Matrixyl and copper peptide go on the skin. They barely reach the blood, and that is the point: each is read where it lands. Semax, a fragment of the stress hormone ACTH, raises the brain-growth factor BDNF in rats;57 on the skin, Matrixyl and copper peptide target the fibroblasts that make collagen.

Semax, evidence 2 of 5
Russian clinical use and one fMRI study, n = 52. A blinded stroke or cognition trial outside Russia would move it; none was retrieved, and no study links Semax to ketamine tolerance.
Matrixyl, evidence 2 of 5
Small cosmetic trials on wrinkle depth. A drug-grade randomised trial would move it; none was retrieved.

Copper peptide takes the skin door too. It is GHK, glycine-histidine-lysine, and your blood already carries it: about 200 ng per mL at age 20 and, by an often-repeated figure, 80 by 60.51 It was found in human serum in 1973, keeping liver cells alive in a dish.49 GHK grips one copper ion, and the pair, GHK-Cu, is the copper tripeptide-1copper tripeptide-1, the ingredient-list name for GHK-Cu; as a drug, prezatide copper. C₁₄H₂₃CuN₆O₄⁺, 402.92 g/mol.72 on ingredient lists. Brands also sell GHK with a palmitoyl tail, a 16-carbon fatty acid that helps it into skin: Pal-GHK (Figure 29).73

Intact human skin keeps GHK-Cu out; pierced or thinned skin lets copper through

In cells GHK raises collagen, elastin and glycosaminoglycan output and supports the fibroblasts that build skin;51 on cell studies alone a 2025 review calls it “undoubtedly” an anti-wrinkle ingredient.73 In people the same review finds “a surprising absence of clinical studies” of GHK-Cu or Pal-GHK.73 Skin is the bottleneck. Water-loving GHK-Cu barely enters it, and intact human skin let almost none through in 9 hours.74

How a serum is made probably matters more than the number on its label. Both labs tested GHK-Cu on excised human skin; Hostynek’s used a flow-through diffusion cell, a chamber that holds skin over a fluid and measures what reaches the fluid.

Hostynek’s lab, the one that saw copper cross, applied 0.68%: close to The Ordinary’s 1%, and 1,360 times Good Molecules’ 5 ppm.80 A review adds that a bound metal or a fatty tail helps more get in, and that a peptide this water-loving and unstable is hard to formulate.73 No trial has pitted two formulas against each other on people, so the label’s percentage is the only number a buyer has.

Where the vials come from, and who keeps the margin

Chemists build a peptide like a necklace, threading amino acids one at a time onto a plastic bead in a tank, then cut it loose, wash it and freeze-dry it. A 15-bead peptide is 15 turns of the loop. Biology has a second way: hand the gene to yeast and brew the chain. Vials sold online are typically made on the bead, and a pharmacy pen is usually brewed, then finished by chemistry. Either way the chain is done in days. The same kind of molecule leaves the factory in different envelopes: a branded pen, a pharmacist's vial, or a website's "research only" vial.

Money follows the factories. Novo Holdings paid $16.5 bn for Catalent in December 2024;61 Pfizer paid about $10 bn for Metsera's obesity pipeline in November 2025;62 CordenPharma plans €900 M of GLP-1 peptide capacity.63 Analysts put the 2025 peptide-drug market anywhere from $50 to 101 bn.65,66 At the other end of the market, crypto payments to grey-market vendors rose 159% in one quarter.60

what to do before you buy
  1. Divide before you buy. Compare price per milligram of copper peptide, as Figure 14 does, not price per bottle.
  2. Read the label’s number. “10%” on a Matrixyl bottle is the share of a supplier’s solution.
  3. The legal doors first. For injectables, a draft from the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) lets 503A503A the section of US drug law for pharmacies that compound a medicine for one named patient, on a prescription pharmacies compounding six peptides, which stay unapproved drugs; comments closed 13 November 2026, docket HHS-OASH-2026-0496.14
  4. A seller’s certificate is the seller grading itself. Finnrick publishes outside tests; Penguin’s record there is 6 of 10, on two GLP-1 peptides rather than the skin peptides.84
  5. A GLP-1 under a code name, sold with mixing water, is what FDA warned a seller about in March 2026.85
  6. California reaches further than federal law. On a plain reading, its 2026 synthetic-cannabinoid definition covers ART27.13, one of Everychem’s compounds, though the same section lets the substances be obtained for bona fide research.87
  7. Ask for the trial. Cells say yes and faces have not been asked. Hostynek’s skin test above came from the University of California, San Francisco, across town from the shop that sells Good Molecules.80,82

Most claims here hold in a rat, in one lab, for a molecule and not a vial

Ask any peptide claim which species, how many labs, and what else is in the vial.

Ask the species. Most peptide claims are true — in a rat. The FDA's own standard for ulcerative colitis is a modified Mayo score over at least a year.13 BPC-157’s only trial ran two weeks and reported a composite index its abstract never defined.

Count the labs. A result from one group is a hypothesis with good press. BPC-157’s second group is small and in Taiwan;17 MOTS-c’s human exercise data still come from the discovering lab’s own collaboration.37

Separate the molecule from the vial. The FDA's safety worry about compounded peptides is the roughly 2% of the powder that isn’t the sequence: chains cut short, beads flipped to their mirror image, clumps and bacterial endotoxin.13 A certificate showing 98% purity says nothing about the other 2%. "Research use only" is a sentence on a label, not a test anyone ran.

Keep the wonder. A 16-bead hormone written inside a ribosome gene and read by the nucleus under stress is a new kind of biology, found in 2015.34,36 The class deserves the excitement. Each molecule has to earn it.

Which of the six peptides on the HHS interim access list14 will get its trial first, and who will pay for it?

Vials in the post hold the same kind of molecule as the drugs that worked, and almost none of the proof behind them.

Read the species, count the labs, test the vial.

pill, peptide, antibody: the same questions asked of each

A pill is a key: small, cheap, swallowable, fits one lock, sometimes the wrong one. An antibody is a hand: big, precise, grabs anything on the outside, must be infused, costs a fortune. A peptide is a note: nature's own words, cheap to print, read only by the right cell, but it dissolves in minutes unless you laminate it.

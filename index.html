Frontier biotech in first-class mail

For $54 to $74, a vial of copper peptide powder comes in the post78, labelled “research use only”. So what is in the envelope? Inside is a short chain of amino acids, the kind of message the body already sends. Insulin is one such message; so are glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) and oxytocin.1 Many of these messages work like a key that fits a receptor on a cell, switches it on at tiny (nanomolar) doses, then breaks down into plain amino acids. That last step is the weak spot: a bare peptide lasts minutes in blood and is lost in the gut, so chemists gave semaglutide a fatty tail, and now it lasts a week.18 Which leaves the vial in the post two questions. Does it work in people? And how would you know? This page asks both of every peptide it follows.

A peptide fits a receptor the way the body’s own hormone does

Why a peptide at all? Most drugs are small molecules, built to sit in a pocket, and many of the body’s most important switches have no pocket. GLP-1’s receptor, for one, holds its hormone across a long interface; others are flat, wide faces where one protein meets another. Peptides fit because they are the messages those switches evolved to hold.1,19

Those messages have had a name since 1902, when Emil Fischer joined two glycines and called the product a peptide, after peptone, digested protein, from the Greek for "cooked" (standard history, not retrieved here). Read literally, the name says what it is: a piece of protein the size digestion leaves.

Digestion cuts one kind of link, the one every peptide is built from. Join the acid end of one amino acid to the amine end of the next and a water molecule leaves; that link is the peptide bond. Each amino acid is now a residue, a bead on a string (bond and term: standard chemistry, not retrieved here). And chemists use peptide loosely for any short chain; the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) draws its legal line at 40.2 With twenty kinds of bead, a 15-bead chain like BPC-157 is one of 2015≈3.3×1019 possible messages.

Each peptide’s effect begins at its own address in the cell

Each of those messages needs an address. Picture every cell as a house, every door an address with its own lock, and peptides as keys the body cuts itself. Some knock at the door, as GLP-1 does at its receptor. Instead of knocking, some hold the furniture still: thymosin β4 grips actin, the cell’s scaffolding,94 and elamipretide sticks to cardiolipin, a fat in the mitochondrial membrane. Some walk right in: MOTS-c, which the mitochondria write themselves, gets as far as the kitchen (the mitochondrion), then the office (the nucleus). And some catch a bus, the blood protein albumin, and stay in town for a week. However it gets in, every visit starts a mechanism of action: the chain from where a drug binds to the change a body shows. Semaglutide’s chain ends in a long programme of randomised trials, BPC-157’s runs mostly through rats, and MOTS-c’s rests on one trial of a close copy, in 20 people. Every one of them starts at an address on this cell.

GLP-1 drugs worked in people, and the money followed

Start with semaglutide’s class, the one that worked. After a meal, your gut texts “enough”. That text is GLP-1, a hormone that slows the stomach and makes you feel full, and tells the pancreas to release insulin only when blood sugar is high.19 Then it self-deletes, in about a minute and a half.95 Chemists shielded it from that enzyme, added the fatty tail from the opening, and a minute and a half became a week.18 First came a drug from a Gila-monster venom peptide (1992);25 semaglutide and tirzepatide followed, and in 2026 retatrutide reached 28% weight loss.24,28 Money came with them: in 2025 those drugs earned Novo 206 bn Danish kroner (DKK) and Lilly $36.5 bn. Researchers followed this chain into people; every chapter here is measured against it.

Mitochondria write a hormone, MOTS-c; only a copy reached a human trial

Lower down the ladder, the biology gets stranger. Mitochondria, the cell’s power plants, keep a tiny notebook of their own DNA. In 2015, on a page everyone had taken for scaffolding, the 12S rRNA gene, researchers found a hidden 16-bead message, MOTS-c.34 Exercise prints it. In the cell’s fluid it brakes the folate cycle, which switches on AMPK, the cell’s fuel gauge, so muscle burns fuel.34,35 When glucose runs short, it walks to the office, the nucleus, and helps switch on antioxidant genes;36 it also travels in the blood, and in mice it falls with age and helps insulin work.34,37

Old mice give the clearest result: at 22 months, treated ones ran 2.16 times as far on a treadmill as untreated (n = 19 and 18).37,38 People have had one trial, of a close copy, CB4211. Even so, MOTS-c matters. It is the clearest case of mitochondria talking back to the nucleus with peptides, the one shown to enter it,52,53 and it turns exercise into a drug target with a messenger the body already makes. For now that’s a research programme, not yet a product.

BPC-157 is a stomach-protein fragment tested almost only in rats

Already a product, BPC-157 is the chain turning at the top of this page: a 15-bead message cut from a stomach protein. It appears to work from outside cells: on blood-vessel cells it raises VEGFR2, the receptor for a growth signal, so vessels grow;29 on tendon cells it raises the growth-hormone receptor, so tendons knit.31,30 Nearly every paper on it since 1992 came from one lab, in Zagreb,17 and sellers pitch it for tendons, gut and joints. Its rodent record is large and consistent. In people, the one fair test (53 patients, two weeks) heard nothing above the noise.13

In numbers, the human record is thin. PubMed holds about 200 BPC-157 studies,17 of which the FDA found five done in people, the only randomised one null and published only as an abstract.13 One tolerability study against placebo, 24 healthy subjects. Three clinic reports, 2 to 17 people each, no control group.17 FDA’s adverse-event database held three reports to December 2025. It found no cancer study at all, and the repeat-dose and DNA-damage studies it did find were in animals, none covering how the compounded product would be used.13 So what can rats tell you? That the mechanism is worth a trial. Not that a tendon heals.

who says BPC-157 works, and who gains if it does?

With no positive trial on file, FDA scientists found the legal criteria "weigh against" adding BPC-157 to the compounding list: no evidence it works in ulcerative colitis, too little human safety data to characterise the risk, a risk of immune reactions to impurities and clumps in the powder (certificates at 97.8% and 98.3% purity, no endotoxin test), and animal toxicology that does not cover the product as it would be sold.13 Its advisory committee voted the other way on 23 July 2026,16 and the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) put BPC-157 on its enforcement-discretion list for injection under the skin or into muscle, by mouth and on the skin.14 From the clinic, a 2025 review of muscle and joint use reached the same place: promising animal data, no controlled human trial.33

Who would gain from a good trial? In February 2026 Undark and STAT reported that Sikirić has been named on BPC-157 patent applications since 1989. He is listed as an owner of PharmaCotherapia, which sponsored a 2015 trial whose data were submitted and then withdrawn from the registry, and as CEO of Diagen, which says it owns a patent on a stable form that is "currently for sale". None of this appeared on the papers they reviewed.17 Conflicts don’t make the biology wrong. They make a second lab worth wanting, and there is one, small, in Taiwan.

Beyond any lab, sport has ruled too: the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) named BPC-157 as an example under S0, non-approved substances, from 1 January 2022.10

On the skin or up the nose, a peptide is read only where it lands

Tendon or gut, a needle is the usual price of a peptide. Semax skips it up the nose, Matrixyl and copper peptide on the skin; they barely reach the blood and need not: each is read where it lands. Semax is a fragment of the stress hormone ACTH, and in rats it raises the brain-growth factor BDNF;57 the other two target the fibroblasts that make collagen.

Copper peptide raises collagen output in cells; intact skin barely lets it in

In cells, GHK raises output of collagen, elastin and glycosaminoglycans and supports the fibroblasts that build skin.51 On cell studies alone, a 2025 review calls it “undoubtedly” an anti-wrinkle ingredient;73 in people, the same review finds “a surprising absence of clinical studies” of GHK-Cu or Pal-GHK.73 Skin is the bottleneck: water-loving GHK-Cu barely gets in, and intact human skin let almost none through in 9 hours.74

So how a serum is made probably matters more than the number on its label. Both labs tested GHK-Cu on excised human skin; Hostynek’s used a flow-through diffusion cell, a chamber that holds skin over a fluid and measures what gets through.

That lab, the one that saw copper cross, applied 0.68%, close to The Ordinary’s 1% and 1,360 times Good Molecules’ 5 ppm.80 A review adds that a bound metal or a fatty tail helps more get in, and that a peptide this water-loving and unstable is hard to formulate.73 No trial has pitted two formulas against each other on people, so a buyer has one number: the percentage on the label. And a percentage is not a trial.

Made in days, a peptide chain leaves the factory in different envelopes

Vials sold online typically start life as a necklace. Chemists thread amino acids one at a time onto a plastic bead in a tank, then cut the chain loose, wash it and freeze-dry it;107,108 a 15-bead peptide is fifteen turns of the loop. Biology has a second way, the one used for human insulin: hand the gene to a living cell and let it brew.107 A pharmacy pen is usually brewed, then finished by chemistry. Either way the chain is done in days (typical times, marked as estimates in Figure 15). What differs is the envelope: the same kind of molecule can leave the factory as a branded pen, a pharmacist’s vial or a website’s “research only” vial. Which envelope is the vial in the post?

Money follows every envelope, factories first. Novo Holdings paid $16.5 bn for Catalent in December 2024;61 Pfizer paid about $10 bn for Metsera's obesity pipeline in November 2025.62 Meanwhile, CordenPharma committed about €900 M over three years to peptide capacity, on GLP-1 contracts it puts at €3 bn.106,63 Analysts put the 2025 peptide-drug market anywhere from $50 to 101 bn.65,66 At the other end, crypto payments to grey-market vendors went from $12 M to $32 M in one quarter, a 159% jump.60,105

Most claims here hold in a rat, in one lab, for a molecule, not a vial

So how would you know? Ask any peptide claim three things: which species, how many labs, and what else is in the vial.

Ask the species. Most peptide claims are true — in a rat. Human trials have to meet a standard: for ulcerative colitis the FDA’s is a modified Mayo score over at least a year.13 BPC-157’s ran two weeks and reported a composite index its abstract did not define.

Then count the labs. One group’s result is a hypothesis with good press. BPC-157’s second group is small, and in Taiwan;17 even MOTS-c’s human exercise data come from the discovering lab’s own collaboration.37

Last, separate the molecule from the vial. What worries the FDA is the roughly 2% of a compounded powder that isn’t the sequence: chains cut short, beads flipped to their mirror image, clumps and bacterial endotoxin.13 Certificates showing 98% purity say nothing about the other 2%. Neither does “research use only”: printing it takes no test at all. No label can tell you what is in any one vial, and this page cannot either. Nothing can, short of opening it and testing.

Testing need not cost the wonder. MOTS-c, a 16-bead hormone written inside a ribosome gene and read by the nucleus under stress, is a new kind of biology, found only in 2015.34,36 As a class, peptides deserve the excitement; each molecule has to earn it.

Which of the six peptides on the HHS interim access list14 will get its trial first, and who will pay for it?

Until someone pays for that trial, the vial in the post holds the same kind of molecule as the drugs that worked, and almost none of the proof behind them. Chemists can make the molecule in days; the proof is a chain of evidence, from cell to rat, rat to person, one lab to a second, and for most of these peptides nobody has finished it.

Read the species, count the labs, test the vial.
